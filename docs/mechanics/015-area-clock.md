# Area scroll clock, the 16→7 area loop, and the near-end death checkpoint

- Mechanic: AREA-01 — one monotonic scroll position advanced each tick before the object walk and carried from area to area within a life; it completes an area at scroll row 0x0E, advances the area number with the 16→7 loop, (discharging the obligation deferred by PLY-02) applies the near-end checkpoint to the death reset scope, and (slice 20) drives the visual terrain, rendered from the arcade map.
- Derived behavior: One scroll clock moves continuously through the game, set to the area top only when a life begins. When the clock's high byte (the "scroll row") reaches 0x0E the area completes: the area number increments, and completing area 16 continues at area 7 (not area 1, no win screen); the clock keeps running, so the new area's map rows simply start arriving. Every new life restarts the current area from its top — except a death in roughly the final fifth of an area (scroll row 0x0E–0x43, read after the death's explosion) advances to the next area instead. A new game starts at area 1. The terrain is the arcade's own map, at the area's map column, scrolled by the clock.

- Scratch interpretation: The sole position authority is `area progress`, a Stage variable stepped by 32 per build tick (16 counter units/arcade frame × 2 frames/tick). Completion requires row 0x0E **and** `area progress` > 0 (the arcade's two-phase wait), and changes `area progress` by −65536 (65056 → −480: the same counter value, still row 0x0E) instead of setting it to 0, so the clock carries on into the next area and the guard keeps the 8 carry ticks on row 0x0E from completing it again. `_enter_area_top` is split along the source routines: `_enter_next_area` (terrain column, schedule cursor, fired flag) runs at completion and at every re-top; completion first hands the outgoing terrain column to `previous terrain column`, and only the re-top (cold start, new game, new life, the two-player handoff) also sets `area progress` to 0, snaps the row, sets `previous terrain column` to −1 and clears the wave registers. Area 1 therefore lasts 2033 ticks and every area reached by completion 2048. The position is still monotonic within a life; the −65536 is a change of origin paired with the area number advancing, not a rewind. The arcade scroll row is DERIVED once per tick into `scroll row` = `floor(((0x0D00 − area progress) mod 0x10000) / 256)` (built through the centralized operator helpers, so the operand slot keys cannot silently drift to NaN); because 256 is divisible by 32, every row is visited and no schedule trigger is skipped. A new warp block `advance area` runs from the walk thread BEFORE `advance slots`; each tick it consumes (AREA-02's) schedule for the row first and then tests completion, the reference's order (the sub CPU runs `handle_objects` before `handle_next_area` every frame). On the tick the completion guard above holds it advances the area (a shared 16→7 helper), carries `area progress`, and runs `_enter_next_area`. (Until slice 21 this record gave the order the other way round and made completion and consume the two arms of one `if/else`, so a record on the completion row never fired; corrected by [record 056](056-release-fidelity.md) item 12.) The near-end checkpoint lives in a SEPARATE Stage `director reset` receiver `area_reset` (kept off `stage_reset`'s pinned opcode chain, like the eight existing reset receivers): on cold-start/new-game it returns to area 1 and re-tops; on a new life it applies the checkpoint below, then re-tops. On a scope-`none` transition and on game-over it does nothing. **The checkpoint (slice 20; reworked in slice 21 by #158, [record 056](056-release-fidelity.md) item 2):** the arcade keeps scrolling through the 88-frame explosion and pause, with completion live, and reads the row only then. Since #158 the port does the same — the hit opens a 44-tick window in `playing`, so the walk keeps scrolling and completing areas through it, and the death transition runs only at the window's end. One shared warp proc `_area_checkpoint` (used by the new-life re-top and the two-player handoff) then applies the arcade's test (row − 14 < 54, i.e. [0x0E, 0x43]) to the live row. (Slice 20 kept a frozen screen and projected the death-tick progress 44 ticks ahead into a `checkpoint progress` register; that projection and the register are retired.) Measured from the hit, the band therefore begins at death-tick progress 50080; a death 37–44 ticks before the end skips the next area too; a death in the carry window restarts the new area. The handoff puts the clock at the area top after the swap, so the incoming player's re-top reads row 7 and leaves their area alone.

  **Terrain (slice 20).** `tools/terrain_render.py` decodes the map ROMs and background tiles at the pin into a committed master map (`assets/terrain/arcade_map.png`, 1024×2048, 1 px per arcade px) and the forest filler, proved against the reference's video-memory snapshot (38 rows at area 9, column 42), and slices them into six costumes: four 64-row bands (plus 2 overlap rows on top), band 3 with its never-written row 255 transparent (for re-tops), and the filler. The two existing strips keep their names: `area_01a` shows the even bands and the filler, `area_01b` the odd bands and the restart band. A Stage warp proc `update terrain` computes each strip's costume, x, y, shown flag and which strip is in front as a pure function of `area progress`, `terrain column` and `previous terrain column` (−1 = filler after a re-top; the outgoing column after a completion, which band 0 keeps while the old area's last rows are on screen). The walk runs it after the clock and the ground objects move, every re-top runs it last, and each broadcasts `terrain draw`, on which a strip only switches costume, goes to x/y, shows or hides, goes to back, and comes forward one layer if it holds the upper band. Size 125 is set only in the target records (scratch-vm caps a block-set size at 1.5× the stage). The phase between the map and the ground sprites is derived from the reference renderer's terms (they cancel to 0) and pinned as named constants, so an object scheduled at row S rides the top edge of map row S − 2 on every tick, matching the arcade.

- Known deviations or uncertainty: `advance area` before `advance slots` locks the PHASE order the enemy slices inherit, not a random-stream draw sequence, so the lock is verified structurally. The slice-5 completion at 65056 versus the spec's former "0xFF00" span is resolved: the spec now gives 0xFE10 steps for an area entered at a life's start and 0x10000 for one entered by completion. **Resolved in slice 21 (#158):** the frozen picture during the death explosion, recorded here as a divergence in slice 20, is gone — the world keeps scrolling through the 44-tick window, as the arcade's does ([record 056](056-release-fidelity.md) item 2). **Divergence for the owner to decide (not a Scratch necessity):** the Amiga reference shifts its foreground playfield 2 px against the ground tiles (`amiga.68k` 981–985), and that playfield carries the sprites as well as the foreground tiles, so in the reference every sprite sits 2 px off the ground. Its comment says the shift reproduces an arcade foreground-versus-background tile offset, so moving the sprites may be a side effect of where the Amiga draws them rather than an arcade fact. The port does not apply it: Namco's purpose-built two-dome clearings centre their objects to the pixel without it (a 2-px shift misses them all, `tests/test_terrain_render.py`). Applying it would be one constant. **Scratch necessities, recorded:** (1) Scratch pushes a sprite back on stage when less than 15 units of it would show, so an entering band is only shown once 15 units are on stage; for at most 13 lines as each area's new rows first enter, the top of the field shows the lower band's overlap rows (the previous area's column) instead. (2) The costumes are 1 costume px per arcade px (bitmapResolution 1) drawn at size 125; if they look soft at playtest they can be re-rendered at resolution 2.
