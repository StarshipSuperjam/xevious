# Arcade screen proportions — one 1.25 scale, a centred arcade window, cabinet bezel panels, and the text grid

- Mechanic: PRES-01 (playfield framing), second half — the owner's choice to frame the game as the cabinet did. The whole 224×288 arcade screen is drawn at **one isotropic scale of 1.25 stage units per arcade pixel**, as a 280×360 play window centred on the 480×360 stage, and the two 100×360 side areas carry **cabinet bezel side panels**. The game runs unchanged inside the window: the HUD and every attract, title, high-score, and entry text sit on the arcade's own text-layer cells inside the window, never over the bezel. This supersedes [record 053](053-playfield-framing.md)'s borderless stage, stop lines, and row-0 cut (same PR), and [record 007](007-spatial-factor-and-movement.md)'s independently tuned movement constants.
- Derived behavior: (1) **Render map.** An object's position is the top-left corner of its 16-px sprite: the craft clamp X 144–304 / Y 16–224 holds a corner-anchored 16-px sprite exactly flush against both side edges (Y 16 and 224 + 16 = 240) and the bottom edge (X 304 + 16 = 320, the last visible line). So its centre is (X+8, Y+8), and with the visible screen spanning depth X 32–320 (rows 4–39) and lateral Y 16–240: stage x = (128 − (Y+8)) × 1.25 = 150 − 10·col, stage y = 180 − (X+8−32) × 1.25 = 210 − 10·row, where col/row are slot units ÷ 256. Depth increases **down** the screen and lateral Y increases to the **left**. (2) **The mirror fix.** The earlier port drew lateral Y increasing to the right, so pressing right moved the craft right only because the input was also inverted; every object, formation, homing turn, and composite was mirrored left-for-right against the arcade. The arcade's right-hand direction entry subtracts from Y, and the display mirrors Y before drawing, so the port now maps +Y to the left and reads right as −Y — the Andor Genesis armour and gun-port lateral offsets are now stored in the arcade's own sense (the top-left plate adds +Y). (3) **Craft.** Moves 1 arcade pixel per frame in depth, 1.5 per frame purely sideways and 1 per frame on a diagonal — 2.5, 3.75, and 2.5 stage units per tick — clamps to stage y 30 (top) … −170 (bottom) and x ±130, and spawns at (0, −160). A move is diagonal only when exactly one of up/down is held: the arcade's joystick cannot report up and down together (`dir_delta_tbl` has no such entry), so the port treats both held as cancelling, and the sideways move then takes the full 3.75 step. (4) **Shot.** Moves 6 pixels per frame, 15 stage units per tick. Rows 0–3 now lie above the stage top (row 0's centre is y 210), where Scratch's sprite fence would hold a sprite back on the stage — so a shot driven by its sprite's y would stall above y 180 and never expire, and the weapon would die after three shots. The shot therefore keeps its depth in a sprite-local variable that drives its travel, its slot mirror, its row-0 expiry, and the Bacura bounce; the sprite only follows it and is hidden once its centre passes the window top (y 180). Until then the stage edge clips its upper half, as the monitor's top edge clipped the arcade shot. Its hit reach still runs to row 0, as record 053 established. (5) **World visibility gate.** Rows 4–39, the arcade's visible rows (record 053's row-0 cut, deviation 2 there, is retired — rows 0–3 are now genuinely off screen). Lateral overhang is masked by the opaque bezel panels drawn in front of the world band, as the monitor's edge masked it. (6) **Sprite sizes.** Every 16-px reference-graphics sprite draws at 125% (its arcade size × 1.25); the explosion phase at 250; the Andor parts at 125; the enemy bullet at 50; the preserved baseline sprites (craft, shot, crosshair, bomb target, bomb, craft explosion) are rescaled by 1.25 / 2.25 at run time so each keeps its proportions. The craft explosion's widest frame reaches 8.3 units either side of its centre, so even at the x ±130 stops it ends at ±138.3, inside the ±140 window. (7) **Composites.** With one scale on both axes the Boza Logram and Andor Genesis depth-offset factor ([record 042](042-boza-logram.md)) is 1 — the offsets are the raw arcade values. (8) **Terrain strips** (the prototype art PR-3 replaces) scroll at one arcade pixel a tick at the render scale, 1.25 units, matching the ground objects; the 690-unit cycle is 552 steps and the pair stays half a cycle apart. (9) **HUD on the text grid.** The reference's text layer is 8×8-pixel cells, visible columns 4–31 and rows 0–35; at 1.25 a cell is 10 units, centred at x = 10·col − 175, y = 175 − 10·row, with characters advancing to the right. HUD glyphs draw at 10 units (size 80), the life icon at 62.5, the whole-string attract text at 58.82 (its 17-px glyph advance onto the 10-unit pitch). Placements, from the reference's screen offsets (MSB = 31 − column, LSB = row): 1UP label column 7 row 0; HIGH SCORE label column 13 row 0; 2UP label column 26 row 0; player score from column 4, high score from column 14, the other player's score from column 23, all row 1; life icons from column 4 on row 35, one column apart; GAME OVER from column 14 row 24 and the two-player banner on row 24; CREDIT at column 22 row 35 with its digits at columns 29–30; PUSH START BUTTON column 10 row 23; INSERT COIN column 13 row 28; the title selector's "1 PLAYER" on the arcade's player-count line, column 10 row 25 (where the arcade writes ONE PLAYER ONLY / ONE OR TWO PLAYERS), with the port's own "2 PLAYERS" on row 26; the best-five table ranks at column 6, scores from column 11, names from column 20, rows 24–32 every second row; CONGRATULATIONS column 10 row 9 and ENTER YOUR INITIALS column 9 row 12; the hidden credit's two lines centred between the arcade's easter-egg rows 33 and 34, its longest (20-character) line on columns 8–27. Each whole-string costume is placed by its centre, `x = 10·(first column + (characters − 1)/2) − 175`. The title logo is sized to 200 units wide (its 20 arcade columns × 10) and glides so the logo body's centre lands at y 50 (anchor y 16): the baked-in "START SPACE KEY" line below the body is left out of the centring.

- Known deviations or uncertainty: (1) **Shot hit window kept doubled (pending owner call; an `ENGINE-TODO` at `HIT_WINDOW_SHOT_FLYING`).** The doubled window ([player craft and weapons](../spec/player-craft-and-weapons.md), WPN-01) was sized for a 2.5-cell-per-tick shot and 36-px sprites; at the arcade's 1.5 cells per tick and arcade-size sprites (a Toroid now draws 20 units across) it is now more forgiving than the drawn body. Kept unchanged so offence feel does not shift silently; the owner decides at playtest. (2) **Collisions sample once per tick.** The harness and the build both advance two arcade frames per tick, so per-frame speeds are pinned structurally in pytest (the harness cannot observe a single frame). (3) **Life-icon cap 28.** The arcade draws one icon per remaining craft with no cap; 28 is the visible width (columns 4–31), past which icons would run off screen. (4) **Leading zeros (pre-existing).** The arcade blanks a score's leading zeros; the port shows them. (5) **1UP flash cadence (pre-existing).** 15 ticks each way, a placeholder; not reconciled here. (6) **Two-player banner on one row.** The arcade draws its two-player game-over text on two rows — GAME OVER on row 24 and PLAYER ONE/TWO from column 13 on row 26 (`display_game_over_player_1_2`); the port's single banner costume sits on row 24, flush right (columns 14–31), so it reads off-centre. The two-row split is the faithful option, offered to the owner. (7) **Port-original layouts.** The title selector's "2 PLAYERS" line (column 10, row 26) — the arcade takes the player count from which start button is pressed, so it has no second line — and the initials-entry layout (the PLAYER-n tag at column 14 row 18, the ten cells on row 24 from column 13) have no arcade counterpart cells and are placed on the grid by choice. (8) **Two-player HUD arrangement.** The active player's score stays on the left (the earlier simplification); the other player's score is on the arcade's 2UP column. (9) **Shot above the window.** While the shot is on rows 0–3 it is hidden but still hit-tested, as in record 053; the sprite hides only once its centre passes y 180, so its upper half is clipped by the stage edge first, as on the monitor. (10) **Feel.** The arcade speeds are much slower than the earlier port-tuned ones: bottom to top takes about 2.7 s, side to side about 2.3 s, and a shot reaches the top in about 0.75 s; the old craft moved ~3.5× faster in depth, and objects now cross the screen about 20% faster relative to its size. The owner may veto at playtest (the alternative is a recorded port speed-up). (11) **Fence-dependent presentation** (what a sprite does at the stage edge) is invisible to the headless harness, which has no renderer — it is an operator-playtest check. (12) **Text strings differ from the arcade's.** The port's PUSH START drops the arcade's BUTTON (it sits on the arcade's cell, centred on its own length); the port writes CONGRATULATIONS in 15 characters where the arcade writes 16 (a trailing mark), and ENTER YOUR INITIALS in 19 where the arcade writes ENTER YOUR INITIAL in 18 — so the sub-header ends one column further right. (13) **Hidden-credit line pitch.** The credit costume's line spacing is 10.9 units against the 10-unit grid, so each line sits about half a unit off its row. (14) **Logo row.** The arcade's logo is drawn on the scrolling background layer, so its resting row is not a fixed text cell; the port's resting y 16 centres the body in the gap above PUSH START and is not grounded to a single reference row. (15) **Start-key hint now unreadable.** The baked-in "START SPACE KEY" line under the logo scales with the logo to about 4.6 units tall; redrawing it as a grid line would be new project text and is left to the owner. (16) **Text legibility.** Whole-string text is resampled twice (once when the costume is rendered, again at its run-time size), so small text can look soft; rendering costumes at their final size is a possible follow-up.
