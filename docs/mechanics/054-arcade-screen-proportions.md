# Arcade screen proportions — one 1.25 scale, a centred arcade window, cabinet bezel panels, and the text grid

- Mechanic: PRES-01 (playfield framing), second half — the owner's choice to frame the game as the cabinet did. The whole 224×288 arcade screen is drawn at **one isotropic scale of 1.25 stage units per arcade pixel**, as a 280×360 play window centred on the 480×360 stage, and the two 100×360 side areas carry **cabinet bezel side panels**. The game runs unchanged inside the window: the HUD and every attract, title, high-score, and entry text sit on the arcade's own text-layer cells inside the window, never over the bezel. Every hit box and the bomb crosshair now work on the arcade's own position units, read from the craft's exact position. This supersedes [record 053](053-playfield-framing.md)'s borderless stage, stop lines, and row-0 cut (same PR), [record 007](007-spatial-factor-and-movement.md)'s independently tuned movement constants, and the half-pixel hit-window units of records [025](025-blaster-to-air-hit.md), [026](026-enemy-bullets-and-collision-death.md), [037](037-bacura-slab.md), [038](038-bacura-bounce.md), and [044](044-secrets.md).
- Derived behavior: (1) **Render map.** An object's position is the top-left corner of its 16-px sprite: the craft clamp X 144–304 / Y 16–224 holds a corner-anchored 16-px sprite exactly flush against both side edges (Y 16 and 224 + 16 = 240) and the bottom edge (X 304 + 16 = 320, the last visible line). So its centre is (X+8, Y+8), and with the visible screen spanning depth X 32–320 (rows 4–39) and lateral Y 16–240: stage x = (128 − (Y+8)) × 1.25 = 150 − 10·col, stage y = 180 − (X+8−32) × 1.25 = 210 − 10·row, where col/row are slot units ÷ 256. Depth increases **down** the screen and lateral Y increases to the **left**. (2) **The mirror fix.** The earlier port drew lateral Y increasing to the right, so pressing right moved the craft right only because the input was also inverted; every object, formation, homing turn, and composite was mirrored left-for-right against the arcade. The arcade's right-hand direction entry subtracts from Y, and the display mirrors Y before drawing, so the port now maps +Y to the left and reads right as −Y — the Andor Genesis armour and gun-port lateral offsets are now stored in the arcade's own sense (the top-left plate adds +Y). (3) **Craft.** Moves 1 arcade pixel per frame in depth, 1.5 per frame purely sideways and 1 per frame on a diagonal — 2.5, 3.75, and 2.5 stage units per tick — clamps to stage y 30 (top) … −170 (bottom) and x ±130, and spawns at (0, −160). A move is diagonal only when exactly one of up/down is held: the arcade's joystick cannot report up and down together (`dir_delta_tbl` has no such entry), so the port treats both held as cancelling, and the sideways move then takes the full 3.75 step. (4) **Shot.** Moves 6 pixels per frame, 15 stage units per tick. Rows 0–3 now lie above the stage top (row 0's centre is y 210), where Scratch's sprite fence would hold a sprite back on the stage — so a shot driven by its sprite's y would stall above y 180 and never expire, and the weapon would die after three shots. The shot therefore keeps its depth in a sprite-local variable that drives its travel, its slot mirror, its row-0 expiry, and the Bacura bounce; the sprite only follows it and is hidden once its centre passes the window top (y 180). Until then the stage edge clips its upper half, as the monitor's top edge clipped the arcade shot. Its hit reach still runs to row 0, as record 053 established. (5) **World visibility gate.** Rows 4–39, the arcade's visible rows (record 053's row-0 cut, deviation 2 there, is retired — rows 0–3 are now genuinely off screen). Lateral overhang is masked by the opaque bezel panels drawn in front of the world band, as the monitor's edge masked it. (6) **Sprite sizes.** Every 16-px reference-graphics sprite draws at 125% (its arcade size × 1.25); the explosion phase at 250; the Andor parts at 125; the enemy bullet at 50; the preserved baseline sprites (craft, shot, crosshair, bomb target, bomb, craft explosion) are rescaled by 1.25 / 2.25 at run time so each keeps its proportions. The craft explosion's widest frame reaches 8.3 units either side of its centre, so even at the x ±130 stops it ends at ±138.3, inside the ±140 window. (7) **Composites.** With one scale on both axes the Boza Logram and Andor Genesis depth-offset factor ([record 042](042-boza-logram.md)) is 1 — the offsets are the raw arcade values. (8) **Terrain strips.** Superseded in the visual-terrain slice (`area.visual-terrain`, [015](015-area-clock.md)): the prototype strips and their 690-unit cycle are gone; the strips now show the arcade map, positioned from the area clock every tick at the 1.25 render scale (size 125 in the target records), so they still move one arcade pixel a tick with the ground objects. (9) **HUD on the text grid.** The reference's text layer is 8×8-pixel cells, visible columns 4–31 and rows 0–35; at 1.25 a cell is 10 units, centred at x = 10·col − 175, y = 175 − 10·row, with characters advancing to the right. Every text costume — the HUD glyphs, the two-player banner, the whole-string attract text and the hidden credit — is rendered at its drawn size, one 20-pixel column per character at bitmap resolution 2, and draws at size 100, so each character steps exactly one 10-unit column (since `presentation.audio-animation`; deviation 16); the life icon draws at 62.5. Placements, from the reference's screen offsets (MSB = 31 − column, LSB = row): 1UP label column 7 row 0; HIGH SCORE label column 13 row 0; 2UP label column 26 row 0; player score from column 4, high score from column 14, the other player's score from column 23, all row 1; life icons from column 4 on row 35, one column apart; GAME OVER from column 14 row 24 and the two-player banner on row 24; CREDIT at column 22 row 35 with its digits at columns 29–30; PUSH START BUTTON column 10 row 23; INSERT COIN column 13 row 28; the title selector's "1 PLAYER" and "2 PLAYERS" left-aligned with each other from column 14 on rows 25 and 26 — row 25 is the arcade's player-count line (where it writes ONE PLAYER ONLY / ONE OR TWO PLAYERS) — so the nine-column pair (columns 14–22, centre column 18) sits centred under PUSH START BUTTON (columns 10–26, centre 18); the best-five screen as the arcade lays it out (`flash_logo_and_high_score_table` shows the logo with the table): the title logo at its resting place above, a header centred on the arcade's header run (row 21, an 18-column run from column 9), ranks 1ST–5TH at column 8, scores from column 13, names from column 22, rows 24–32 every second row — the arcade's rank, score, and name columns 6, 11, and 20 shifted two columns right, the furthest a full 10-letter name still fits (it ends on column 31), so a row with three-letter initials sits within about a letter of centre; CONGRATULATIONS column 10 row 9 and ENTER YOUR INITIALS column 9 row 12; the hidden credit's two lines centred between the arcade's easter-egg rows 33 and 34, its longest (20-character) line on columns 8–27. Each whole-string costume is placed by its centre, `x = 10·(first column + (characters − 1)/2) − 175`. The title logo (since slice 21, [record 056](056-release-fidelity.md) item 10) is the arcade's own tile art, 20 columns × 8 rows on screen columns 8–27 and rows 10–17 (`display_xevious_logo_flashing`'s background offsets name rows 9–16; the title's layer scroll draws them one row lower), drawn at the sprite scale so one 8-px character fills one 10-unit cell, centred on that block at (0, 40) and drawn in place from the first frame; the port's "START SPACE KEY" hint is its own text run on row 20 from column 11. (10) **Hit boxes in the arcade's units.** Every collision compares two sprites' "shadow" bytes (`osd_update_sprite_shadow`): byte 0 is `0xef − (Y >> 5)`, the lateral position in whole arcade pixels, inverted, and byte 1 is `((X >> 5) + 8) >> 1`, the depth position in 2-pixel units — so the port reduces a position to `floor(slot y / 32)` laterally and `floor((slot x + 256) / 64)` in depth. Earlier builds read both bytes as half pixels (16 slot units) on swapped axes, which shrank every box: the bomb's 20 × 20-pixel box was effectively 5 × 10, so a bomb dropped between two ground objects 16 pixels apart missed both. Each window is tested with the reference's idiom `sub.b #bias; add.b #width`, which hits when the byte difference lies in [bias − width, bias − 1]. The boxes are: enemy bullet or flying enemy vs craft, [−8, 7] px lateral × [−4, 3] depth units (16 × 16 px); Bacura vs craft, [−12, 27] × [−8, 7] (the reference box; since the visual-terrain slice the port kills the craft only on the tumble frame's opaque part inside it, an owner-chosen divergence recorded in [037](037-bacura-slab.md)); shot vs flying enemy, [−16, 15] × [−8, 7] (32 × 32 px); shot vs Bacura, [−8, 23] × [−8, 7]; bomb vs ground object, [−10, 9] × [−5, 4] (20 × 20 px); Bonus Flag vs craft, the bomb's box. Each delta is taken in the direction its routine takes it: object minus craft (or minus shot, minus bomb target) on both bytes — the inverted byte 0 turns the routines' `reference − object` into object minus reference in pixels — except the flag, whose depth byte is `craft − flag`. So a bomb midway between two ground objects 16 pixels apart lands 8 pixels from each and destroys both, as the arcade does, while one 11 pixels off misses. The two asymmetric Bacura boxes were also mirrored before (lateral [−28, 11] and [−24, 7]); the carry range puts the tall side where the reference has it. (11) **The craft read is exact.** The craft's position is read every tick as unrounded slot units — depth `(210 − y) / 10 × 256`, lateral `(x − 150) / −10 × 256` — alongside the rounded cell the aim tables still use. The crosshair (`craft depth − 3072`, the 96-pixel lead, at the craft's lateral position), the bomb's drop point, and the craft-side hit boxes all take this exact read, so the crosshair glides with the craft (3.75 units a tick sideways is exactly 96 slot units) instead of jumping a whole 8-pixel cell at a time.
