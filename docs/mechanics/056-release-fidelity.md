# Release fidelity — the last gameplay, art and HUD corrections before the release candidate

- Mechanic: Slice 21, the one release pull request. Each numbered item below is one focused commit. (1) **AREA-02, `area.add-object-dispatch`:** the area schedule's 13 add_object records now place their objects. The hidden bonus flag appears in areas 1, 3, 5 and 7 without the debug key; a Torkan appears in area 3, a Kapi in area 7, a Terrazi in areas 7 and 11, and the Garu Zakato in areas 9, 10 and 14. A record whose slot is already busy is dropped: the arcade writes the type into the slot, but a live object never reads it and clears it when it is freed, so nothing is queued. The Garu Zakato now lives in arcade object slot 0x3B, so its detonation writes into the four slots after it (0x3C–0x3F), as the arcade does: a free slot gets a Brag Spario, and a slot already holding an enemy only has that enemy moved onto the Garu with the Spario's velocity. (2) **PLY-02 / AREA-01, #158 — the world keeps running through the player's explosion.** A hit no longer freezes the screen: the craft explodes in place while the terrain, enemies, bullets and spawners carry on for the arcade's 88 frames, and only then does the death route (respawn, game over or the end of a demo) run. (3) **PLY-02.forest-wait, `player.final-forest-wait`.** After the last craft is lost, the screen shows only forest for 64 frames before the high-score entry, GAME OVER or the other player's turn, as after every arcade death. (4) **DIF-02.ship-divisor, `difficulty.ship-number-divisor`.** The area schedule's score re-tune divides the player's score by the ship number — the craft in play, counted from 1 — instead of the craft left, so a player who scores well while losing few craft meets more pressure and one who has lost many is spared, as in the arcade. (5) **ECO-03.stop-after-two, `economy.stop-after-two` (#103).** The second bonus craft comes at 60,000 points, not 80,000, and the ones after it at every 60,000 (120,000, 180,000 …). Past 9,960,000 the threshold wraps back to 20,000, as the arcade's does, so the next awards grant craft again. The setting that stops after the second bonus craft is recorded as unreachable in this build. (6) **AIR-10.brag-spario-survives, `presentation.reference-art`.** A shot never destroys a Brag Spario. Each hit scores 500 and uses up the shot, and the Spario keeps homing in; it leaves only off the screen. Until this commit the port blew it up with the shared air explosion. (7) **AIR-07.teleport-art, the teleport scatter, `presentation.reference-art`.** A base or Brag Zakato now teleports in mid-field, on a random row 5–20, instead of at the top edge. While it winks in, and again while it self-destructs after firing, it drifts down the screen with the terrain. The sparkle holds still as it shrinks, and the burst jumps one cell up and to the left near its end, both as in the arcade. (8) **CAB-05 / AIR-07, AIR-08, AIR-10, `presentation.reference-art` — the enemies draw the arcade's own sprites.** The Giddo Spario, Brag Spario, Zakato, Brag Zakato and Garu Zakato stop borrowing the Zakato body from the fan rip. The Giddo spins through its four frames as it changes colour, and bursts in its own four frames when shot. The Brag Spario tumbles. The Brag Zakato and Garu Zakato bodies pulse through the arcade's colour cycle; the Zakato body uses none of the pens that cycle changes, so it looks the same throughout, as in the arcade. A Zakato's teleport sparkle and self-destruct burst draw the arcade's frames, and the burst keeps the body's last colour. (9) **CAB-05 / WPN-01, `presentation.reference-art` — the shot draws the arcade's own sprites.** The player's shot stops using its old stand-in sprite: as it flies it alternates between the arcade's two shot pictures in the arcade's two colours, and a shot that strikes a Bacura draws the arcade's four-picture rebound. The rebound now lasts as long as the arcade's — eight arcade frames — instead of twice that, and backs off a quarter of the shot's forward speed for that time. (10) **CAB-01.title-sparkle / CAB-05, `presentation.reference-art` — the title draws the arcade's logo, sparkle and flash.** The title logo is the arcade's own tile art, drawn in place on its own columns and rows from the first frame instead of the fan rip gliding in. After about a second a sparkle twinkles at the left of the letters, sweeps right along their top and fades; then the logo's outline flashes through the arcade's eight colours until the demo starts. A banked credit removes the sparkle and holds the outline in its red, as the arcade's coined-up screen does; the best-five screen shows the red logo and the demo the yellow one. The "START SPACE KEY" hint is now its own line of text under the logo, readable at the text size. The title holds 740 frames, not 744. (11) **ECO-02 / PRES-02, `presentation.hud-yellow` — the HUD draws its numbers and labels as the arcade does.** The scores no longer show leading zeros: a zero score reads 00, and 1230 reads 1230, not 0001230. The 1UP label flashes 16 frames on and 16 off, twice as fast as the old placeholder, and only in a real game. On the title INSERT COIN flashes at the same rate, and it now flashes in the demo too; PUSH START BUTTON, once a coin is in, no longer flashes. The two-player elimination banner is two rows: GAME OVER, and under it PLAYER 1 or PLAYER 2. The best-five table's scores lose their leading zeros as well. The score digits stay white. (12) **AREA-02, the completion tick (found by the soak).** On the tick an area completes, a schedule record on that row now fires before the area advances, as in the arcade. Only one record is affected: area 13's last formation reset, which never fired before, so area 13's last formation carried into the start of area 14.
- Derived behavior: (1) **add_object.** When the area clock reaches an add_object record's row, the record names an object type and one raw arcade object slot: 0x00 for the bonus flag (the ground band's slot 0), 0x3A or 0x3B for the single flyers (the first two formation slots). The arcade writes the type into that slot unconditionally; on the same frame the object pass gives the type its handler only if the slot was idle. If the slot was busy, the live object's handler keeps running and clears the type when that object frees, so the record is lost — never queued or retried. The new object's own init runs on the following frame: the flag takes the craft-excluding random column, hidden and worth 1,000 at reveal; Torkan, Kapi and Garu Zakato take the plain random column; Terrazi takes the craft-excluding one. Because the object pass runs before the formation spawner, a placed single owns its slot before the spawner writes; the spawner writes a type into the first formation slots from 0x3A every frame, idle or not, but a live object ignores it, so the single keeps its slot until it is freed. The Garu Zakato's detonation reads slot 0x3B absolutely and writes the Garu's position and the four Spario velocities into 0x3C–0x3F; only the free ones become Brag Sparios. (2) **The explosion window.** On a hit the flight sound stops and, in a real game only, the explosion sound starts; an attract demo dies silently. The craft's own handler switches to the explosion: seven 8-frame steps, then a 32-frame pause in which the craft is no longer drawn — 88 frames in all. Nothing else stops: the objects, the scroll, the spawners and the enemy bullets are gated only on the scroll being disabled, and that happens only at the window's end, together with the ship number going up. During the window the craft cannot be hit again (the hit test runs only on the live craft's path), cannot move or fire a new shot, no new bomb press is accepted, and the crosshair stays where it was; shots already flying keep moving and scoring, and a bomb already falling keeps falling and scores where it lands, because the bomb is a coroutine that resumes past the press check. The bonus flag's collection test reads the craft's shadow position without asking whether it is exploding, so the craft can collect the flag while its explosion is drawn. In the pause its state is cleared, and the shadow update then zeroes its position: the Amiga layer also parks its shadow off-screen, so it can no longer collect the flag (the Neo Geo layer zeroes the position but leaves the shadow where it was). For those 16 ticks every aim, homing turn and spawn position that reads the craft's position reads 0. When the window ends an attract demo ends; a real game runs the existing death route. The near-end checkpoint then reads the live scroll row (record 015). (3) **The forest wait.** After every real-game death the arcade fills the background with forest and waits 64 frames, and only then looks at the craft left: it continues with the next life, or the game ends (the best-five check, then GAME OVER), or in a two-player game the turn passes. The scroll counter is not moved, so the checkpoint still reads the death row. On the frame the scroll is disabled the object pass starts over, which clears the whole 64-object table, and then idles until the scroll is enabled again; so every enemy, bullet, ground object and the bomb sight vanish as the wait begins, and the wait shows only forest and the score display. A demo ends when the scroll is disabled and never reaches the wait. (4) **The ship number.** Each player has a ship number: 1 when the game starts (both players in a two-player game), one more, as a byte, when each death's 88-frame window ends — an attract demo's included — and swapped with the other player's at the turn change. The re-tune record takes the score's thousands as its four BCD digits read as one binary number (20,000 points is 0x0020 = 32; 999,000 is 0x0999 = 2,457) and divides it by the ship number with the floor. A ship number of 0 — reached only after 255 craft — makes the arcade's loop return 0xFFFF. The quotient is capped at 16 by a signed compare, so a quotient of 0x8000 or more (from 8,000,000 points while still on ship number 1) is not capped. The result is added to the AI level within the byte, with no fold. The level raise likewise adds within the byte before its fold test. (5) **The bonus threshold.** When the score reaches the threshold, a craft is granted and the threshold advances. If the threshold just reached is below the setting's increment, the new threshold is the increment itself; otherwise the increment is added. At the default setting (first 20,000, increment 60,000) that gives 20,000, 60,000, 120,000, 180,000 …. The add is a four-digit BCD add of the thousands that drops its carry, so 9,960,000 + 60,000 becomes 20,000; that wrapped threshold is below the score, so every award grants a craft until the threshold climbs back past it. On the add path only, the arcade first checks the low three bits of the inverted DIP switch byte; the value 6 marks the last bonus, so the craft just granted is the last one. The catch-up path skips that check. The bonus setting itself is bits 2–4 of the same byte, so only bit 2 is shared: the value 6 needs an odd setting index and also bits 0–1 set to 1,0 from the neighbouring switch field, so it is not a bonus setting on its own. (6) **The Brag Spario under fire.** Its handler has no hit branch: it sets `_STATE` back to 2 every frame before it moves. The shot test hits only an enemy whose `_STATE` is 2; a hit sets 3, clears the shot, scores the enemy's points (500 for a Brag) and plays the flying-hit sound. The hit leaves `_CODE` alone, so the body is drawn throughout. Both collision tests read the sprite positions the sub CPU snapshots at the start of each frame, before the object pass moves anything, so a shot meets the Spario where it is drawn; the next frame its handler puts it back to 2. The craft-contact test runs before the handler and skips any enemy not in state 2, so on the frame after a hit the Spario cannot ram the craft. During play it is freed only when it leaves the screen. Its only spawner is the Garu Zakato detonation: type 0x09 is in neither flying-type table and in no schedule record. (7) **The teleport.** `init_teleport` sets the row to `(rnd & 0x0F) + 5`, then draws the column with `gen_random_Y_store_obj` (3–27, no craft reject) and adds 1, so rows 5–20 and columns 4–28. Both Zakato families call it. Every frame of the sparkle the handler calls `zakato_teleport` and, until it completes, `scroll_sprite_X`, which moves the object with the terrain (16 units a frame). At timer 8 the object moves +1 row and −1 column. That move cancels a size change: the sparkle's first two cells are 2×2 sprites (attribute 0x83), which the sprite routine draws 8 px right and down of a 1×1 at the same position, so on screen the sparkle holds still as it shrinks. After firing, `zakato_explode_and_remove` calls `zakato_explode` and `scroll_sprite_X` each frame until the burst ends; at timer 0x10 the object moves −1 row and +1 column. `zakato_explode` builds the size bits but never stores them, so the burst stays 1×1 and this move shows as a one-cell jump up and to the left. The scroll call also removes an object that crosses the bottom or side edge (`check_scroll_offscreen`). The cell `0x104` listed last in the teleport table is never shown, since the sparkle ends at index 5. (8) **The enemy art.** The pulsing colour is one global value, written by the sub CPU every frame unless scroll is disabled: `colour_lut_pulsing_2` indexed by `(countup >> 3) & 7`, the steps 0x10, 0x11, 0x12, 0x13, 0x14, 0x13, 0x12, 0x11, eight frames each. Every Zakato-family handler copies it into its colour each frame it survives, after its hit and fire tests. A Zakato or Brag Zakato that fires on its first live frame therefore keeps the teleport's 0x24 for its burst, and otherwise keeps the colour of its last live frame. The Garu writes it the same way; its init writes no colour. The bodies are codes 0x111 (Zakato), 0x112 (Brag Zakato) and 0x113 (Garu), drawn 1×1. The Zakato body uses none of the pens the five pulsing colours change, and the Brag Zakato at 0x14 draws that same picture, so the build reuses one costume for those. The sparkle is `zakato_teleport_sprite_tbl` in table order, 0x10C and 0x108 as 2×2 then 0x107, 0x106, 0x105, four frames each, at 0x24, flipped by `TIMER & 3`. The self-destruct is codes 0x104–0x108, four frames each, 1×1 and unflipped, because `zakato_explode` stores only the code. It starts on the firing frame: `zakato_shoot` and `brag_zakato_explode` arm `_TIMER = 0xFF` (3762, 3921) and `zakato_explode` increments before it draws (3932), so the burst shows timer 0 first, moves one cell at 0x10 and frees at 20. The port used to arm at 0 and run the burst one tick early; it now arms one step below zero (`ZAKATO_SELF_DESTRUCT_ARM`), and `zakato-fires-once-then-self-destructs` pins timer 0 on the firing tick. The Giddo draws code `0x100 + ((countup >> 1) & 3)` at colour `0x26 + ((countup >> 3) & 3)`; shot, it draws 0x104–0x107, two frames each, without writing its colour. The Brag Spario draws 0x115 at 0x26, flip bits `countup & 0x0C`; on the upright screen bit 3 mirrors left-to-right and bit 2 top-to-bottom. The Neo Geo renderer passes both bits to the hardware; the Amiga renderer tests only bit 3 and drops the flips of a 2×2. (9) **The shot art.** The shot's update writes its look every frame (`main_fn_30_shot_fn` 2372–2388): code 0x116 + ((countup >> 2) & 1), colour 0x23 + ((countup >> 1) & 1), and `_ATTR` bit 3 from countup & 1. On the frame the shot is found at STATE 3, `shot_destroyed` sets the reflected `_dX = 24`, colour 0x23 and `_TIMER = 0xFF`, and yields before moving, so that frame shows the ordinary shot code at 0x23 in place. Each later frame increments the timer, deletes the shot when it reaches 8, and otherwise draws code 0x118 + ((TIMER >> 1) & 3) with bit 3 from TIMER & 1 and moves by 2 × `_dX`. The colour is never rewritten, so the rebound stays 0x23. Eight frames of rebound travel at 48 units against the forward 192 is a quarter of the forward distance per frame. (10) **The title.** `display_xevious_logo_flashing` writes the logo onto the two tile layers: seven strings and one single tile onto the background layer, each cell coloured by its own attribute run, and four outline strings onto the text layer in red 0x1A. The background layer sits 12 px below the text grid at scroll 0 (its buffer is drawn four lines high and its rows start two cells down), and the title's scroll of 4 px (896) reduces that to exactly one text row, so it lands one text row lower than its offsets name (rows 9–16 drawn as 10–17); together the logo covers text columns 8–27 and rows 10–17. The title stage holds 64 frames on its countdown (it runs out on frame 63), then sets up the sparkle (object 0x0F) at `_X` 0xD60, `_Y` 0x1660, colour 0x0F. It appears in place for 15 frames (timer 1–15, code 0x30 + ((timer >> 1) & 7)), moves for 135 (timer 0x79–0xFF, code 0x38 + (timer & 7), `_Y` − 0x20 a frame: 1.25 units right on screen) and disappears for 15 (timer 15–1, the appear codes in reverse), and is removed on frame 228. From then the flash steps a countdown from 0 on every other frame (`countup_timer_1` bit 0), recolouring the outline from `xevious_flashing_logo_colour_tbl` at `countdown & 7` — 7, 6, …, 0, 7 … — so it starts on the table's last colour; the 256th step recolours nothing: the countdown has reached 0 again, so the stage clears the objects and the demo stage begins. With the steps on the odd frames 229, 231, … 739 that is 740 frames from the title's entry to the demo (739 on the other phase, deviation 12), and the last colour shown is the table's index 1. Every frame of the stage also runs `flash_insert_coin_and_check_credits`; with a credit banked it goes to `coined_up`, which clears every object (the sparkle with it) and redraws the logo in red, and the title then waits for START without the sequence. The best-five screen draws the same red logo, and the demo draws a yellow variant (`display_xevious_logo_yellow`, text layer only, colour 0x1B). (11) **The HUD's numbers and labels.** `display_score` writes a score through `display_bcd_value`, which blanks up to five leading zeros: the units and tens always print, and the digit at place p ≥ 2 prints only once the value reaches 10^p, so interior zeros stay. It draws the HUD score, the high score, the other player's score and every best-five score. The active player's nUP label is blanked while bit 4 of the frame counter is set — 16 frames shown, 16 blank — and only while `is_real_game` is set (set at START, cleared on the return to attract), so in the demo it stays lit; the other player's label never flashes. INSERT COIN is drawn while that bit is set and wiped while it is clear, so it flashes on the same 32-frame cycle; the title stage and the demo stage both call it every frame, the best-five stage does not. PUSH START BUTTON is drawn once on `coined_up` and stays. The two-player elimination banner writes GAME OVER on row 24 from column 14 and the player string on row 26 from column 13. (12) **The completion tick.** Each frame the sub CPU runs its function table in index order: the schedule step, which executes the record at the pointer when its row matches the scroll row, and then the area step, which advances the area when the row reaches 0x0E. So on the frame the row first reads 0x0E, a record on row 0x0E executes for the outgoing area, and only then is the pointer moved to the next area's table. Area 13's final record is a flying-formation reset on row 0x0E: it clears the wave count and type offset as area 13 ends, and area 14 sends no formation until its own first record, 15 rows (about 4 seconds) later. The schedule step executes one record per frame, so only one record on the completion row could ever fire; area 13's is the only one.

- Known deviations or uncertainty: (1) **The init runs in the pickup tick.** The arcade picks the type up on one frame and runs the init on the next; the port does both in one walk tick (two frames), after the bomb and before the formation refill. The object can therefore appear up to one frame earlier than in the arcade; it still makes its first move on the following tick. The port also tests the slot once per tick: a record that arrives on the tick its slot is freed is placed, where in the arcade the outcome depends on the order of the two CPUs within the frame. (2) **A bounded position draw.** The arcade's random-column draws always succeed within the frame; the port's shared draw stops after `SPAWN_DRAW_ATTEMPTS` (16) tries, as it already does for the formation spawner, and an object whose draw is exhausted is not placed. This is an existing port necessity (a Scratch tick cannot loop unbounded), shared with every other spawn that uses the draw. (3) **One stop for every sound at the hit (port necessity).** The arcade stops only the flight sound; Scratch can stop only every sound at once, so a start theme still playing early in a life is cut too (record 055 deviation 1). (4) **The demo shows its explosion.** The demo pilot pauses during the window, as the arcade's does (its moves are made inside the input, shooting and bombing handlers, none of which run while exploding), and the demo now runs its 88-frame explosion before it ends where it used to end at the hit — the arcade's behaviour. (5) **The flag enters at `slot x` 0.** That is the top-of-field row every ground object enters at in the port ([record 033](033-ground-bombing-pipeline.md)). The arcade's flag init sets no `_X` (`handle_54_Bonus_Flag` 3131-3136); it keeps the 0 that a freed object's slot is left with, because the shadow update zeroes `_X`/`_Y` when an object goes inactive (`src/amiga/amiga.68k` 1659-1668), so the two agree. (6) **The forest's phase.** The arcade fills the plane where the scroll stopped, so where the forest pattern's rows fall on screen depends on the death row; the port draws the re-top's forest, which always sits at the same height. The pattern repeats, so only its vertical phase differs. (7) **Formation indices past the normal table (port necessity).** The uncapped escape and the zero-divisor case can push the AI level, after the fold, into 0x80–0xBF. The arcade then reads past the normal formation table into the 64 entries stored after it — the super table's lead-in and start (`src/xevious_sub.68k` 451–459). The committed `docs/spec/data/formations.json` is produced by a guarded extractor and stops at index 127, so the port's existing guard keeps the previous formation for those indices. They are reachable only at 8,000,000 points or more without losing a craft, or after 255 craft. (8) **The demo's re-tune adds nothing.** The arcade's demo set-up clears the area and the AI level but neither the score nor `solvalou_number` (`attract_mode_gameplay`, `src/xevious_main.68k` 1298–1324), so a demo's re-tune divides the last game's score by the last game's ship number, carried on by any demo deaths since; before any game has been played the value is whatever the RAM held at power-on (uncertain; a zeroed RAM would make it 0 and each re-tune add 0xFFFF). The port starts every demo through the new-game reset, which zeroes the score and sets the ship number to 1, so a demo's re-tune always adds 0 and its AI level comes from the raises alone. This rides on the port's existing choice to start each demo from a fresh world, and only changes which formations a demo shows. (9) **One shot, one flying hit (pre-existing, raised to the operator).** The arcade's flying shot test checks all six flying slots before it marks the shot spent (`check_flying_enemies_shot` 2516–2545), so one shot through overlapping flying enemies scores every one of them; the four Brag Sparios a Garu Zakato detonation starts on one spot (`init_garu_zakato_explosion` 5075–5103) can pay 4 × 500 to a single shot. The port's shared detector spends the shot on its first hit. This predates slice 21 and affects every flying family, so it is not changed in item 6. (10) **The shot is never drawn mirrored.** The arcade mirrors the flying shot on every odd frame and the rebound on every odd timer step. A port tick draws one of each pair of frames — the even countup — so the flying shot is always upright and the rebound always mirrored; the frames between are never drawn. This is the same two-frames-a-tick sampling every animated sprite in the port uses. (11) **The start-key hint is port text.** The arcade's title has no "START SPACE KEY" line; the port needs one because its controls are keys. It is now its own text run on row 20 and omits the C coin key, as the baseline hint did. (12) **The flash's phase.** The flash steps on the frames where `countup_timer_1` bit 0 is clear; the title does not set that free-running counter, so whether the flash starts on frame 228 or 229, and the title ends a frame later, depends on its phase. The port takes 740 frames (370 ticks), a whole number of ticks; that is the phase whose steps fall on the odd frames 229 … 737 (the 256th, on frame 739, exits), so the outline is red through tick 114 and then on colour (114 − n) mod 8 at tick n (frame 2n). The sparkle is drawn on the even frames, the same two-frames-a-tick sampling as deviation (9). (13) **The background row.** The logo's background layer sits 12 px below the text grid at scroll 0 and the title's scroll of 4 lifts it to exactly one text row below its offsets; the port bakes that shift into the background costume instead of scrolling a layer. (14) **The flash runs on the port's clocks.** The arcade flashes from one free-running frame counter, so the 1UP label and INSERT COIN keep one phase across screens; the port reads the clock each screen already has — `tick` in play and in the demo, `title tick` on the title — so the phase restarts with each screen. The rate and the share of time shown are the arcade's.
