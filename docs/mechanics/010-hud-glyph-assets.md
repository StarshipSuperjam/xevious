# HUD glyph, life icon, and extend-sound assets

- Mechanic: ECO-02 HUD media ingestion — the digit/letter glyph set, the remaining-lives ship icon, and the extend/1UP cue the later HUD-render commit will read.
- Derived behavior: Twenty-three glyph costumes (digits 0-9 and the uppercase letters `1UP`, `2UP`, `HIGH SCORE`, and `GAME OVER` need — A, C, E, G, H, I, M, O, P, R, S, U, V) plus one remaining-lives ship costume are attached to a new hidden `hud` sprite, and the extend/1UP cue is attached to the Stage as a new `extend` sound. This commit is media only: nothing in the generated project reads these costumes or plays this sound yet.
- Reference provenance: The credited HUD font sheet and its per-glyph crop rectangles in `assets/hud-font/manifest.json`; the extend sound sourced from Sounds Spriters Resource asset 449687; the remaining-lives icon cropped from the already-credited Solvalou sheet (`assets/sprite-extraction/manifest.json`), all listed in `docs/ASSET_CREDITS.md`.
- Transfer class: Media.
- Scratch interpretation: A single new hidden, scriptless-this-slice `hud` target holds all 24 costumes (one glyph set; a later commit's clones will spell multi-character labels by switching costumes, never by compositing). The Stage's existing two historical sounds gain one new `extend` entry. `tools/game_director.py` owns the `hud` target's existence and its (currently empty) blocks; `tools/hud_glyphs.py` owns that target's costume list and the Stage's `extend` sound entry, mirroring the existing solvalou split where one generator owns blocks and the other owns costumes.
- Scratch evidence: `hud` in `src/xevious/project.json` (24 costumes, `blocks: {}`, `visible: false`); the Stage's `sounds` list; generated PNGs, the wav, and their records in `src/xevious/assets/`; `tools/game_director.py` (`_ensure_hud_target`, `HUD_TARGET`); `tools/hud_glyphs.py`; `tests/test_hud_glyphs.py`; the Stage-sound assertion in `tests/test_scratch_project.py`'s `test_canonical_source_preserves_untouched_historical_content`.
- Acceptance criteria: Two `hud_glyphs.py generate` runs are byte-identical; the font sheet, life-icon sheet, and extend-sound source hashes stay pinned; every glyph costume is a uniform square RGBA bitmap with transparency, centered on a fixed monospace cell; the life icon keeps its native craft colors on a 16x16 canvas; the extend sound is a valid Stage wav sound entry; `python3 tools/game_director.py check`, `python3 tools/hud_glyphs.py check`, `python3 tools/sprite_extractor.py check`, and `python3 tools/scratch_project.py verify` all pass; no script yet switches a glyph costume or plays the extend sound.
- Fidelity status: Non-gameplay media extraction. The glyph cell geometry (100x100 monospace cell, 4x nearest-neighbor downscale, bitmapResolution 2) and the life-icon crop are project-defined presentation choices, not reference-pinned values; the digit/letter shapes themselves come directly from the credited font sheet.
- License status: The HUD font sheet is Creative Commons Attribution 3.0 (CC BY 3.0) by Patrick H. Lauke (FontStruct), reshared by AnthonyCassimiro — attribution recorded in `docs/ASSET_CREDITS.md` and in `src/xevious/assets/provenance.json`, not treated as a grant beyond that license's terms. The life icon inherits the existing Solvalou sheet's no-reusable-license-specified status (third-party copyrighted material). The extend sound inherits the same no-reusable-license-specified status from Sounds Spriters Resource. The CC BY 3.0 grant covers the FontStruct author's recreation as an expression; the depicted HUD glyph *design* derives from Namco's arcade game and remains Namco's — so the project's standing no-ownership-claim and rights-review-before-distribution (`docs/REFERENCE_POLICY.md`) apply to these glyphs as to all third-party art here.
- Known deviations or uncertainty: The operator-supplied font sheet was described as pure black-on-white with no anti-aliasing; the measured glyph crop rectangles actually contain a small amount of anti-aliasing (intermediate gray shades at glyph edges). The global per-channel >=128 threshold binarizes this deterministically (transparent above the threshold, opaque white below), so the output is still exact and reproducible, but it is a binarization of near-pure art rather than of a strictly two-color source. The source font also draws the letter O and the digit 0 identically, so `glyph/O` and `digit/0` are two costumes that legitimately share one content-addressed PNG asset — not a bug. The `hud` target's blocks are an empty scaffold; HUD rendering (costume selection, positioning, and playing the extend sound on a bonus life) is deferred to the ECO-02 HUD-render commit.
