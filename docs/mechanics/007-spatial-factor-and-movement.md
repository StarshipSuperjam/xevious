# Solvalou movement, shot speed, and the crosshair coupling

- Mechanic: The Solvalou's eight-directional movement and bounds, the blaster shot's forward speed, and the crosshair's forward lead — expressed as **port-tuned constants** (not a single spatial factor), with the crosshair kept on screen by a **craft↔crosshair coupling** rather than by a scale (PLY-01, WPN-01 flight, WPN-03 lead).
- Derived behavior: The craft moves eight-directionally at a fixed per-axis step on each active axis (diagonals un-normalized), spawning/respawning at a fixed point; it is bounded by touching the painted play-area frames, and each frame-edge touch also nudges the crosshair the same way. Shots fly forward at a fixed speed and expire at the top border. The crosshair reads the **same movement input as the craft** and sits a lead ahead of it; when the crosshair reaches the top play-border it clamps there and sends the craft back down with it, so the reticle can never leave the top of the screen and the craft stops with it. This is the validated recovery-build (#13) behavior, preserved unchanged.
- Reference provenance: `jotd666/xevious@71473685a8c7856c8401c8519276cd97a38d4183`; the movement direction rule, the arcade craft-clamp bounds (X 144-304 / Y 16-224), the fixed spawn (296,120), the 6 px/frame shot speed, and the 96 px crosshair lead are the recorded values in [player craft and weapons](../spec/player-craft-and-weapons.md) (PLY-01, WPN-01, WPN-03); the tick conversion (1 build tick = 2 arcade frames) and the port-conversion rules are [core game systems](../spec/core-game-systems.md) (Units section). This record cites those homes and does not restate the arcade constants.
- Transfer class: Numeric constant and port conversion (instruction-derived rule; the stage speeds/bounds/lead are a port mapping of the recorded arcade values onto the 480×360 stage — no source text or media copied).
- Scratch interpretation: The spatial quantities are **independent port-tuned literals**, not derived from one common factor and not read from sprite/costume geometry. The craft moves at `±7` stage units per tick per active axis (`motion_changexby`/`changeyby` gated on `sensing_keypressed`), spawns/respawns at `(0, -85)`, and is bounded by `touching` the `frame_b`/`frame_l`/`frame_r` overlay sprites — each touch corrects the craft and broadcasts `target_b`/`target_l`/`target_r` so the crosshair follows. The blaster clone travels at `DY 20` per tick and expires on `frame_t`. The crosshair (`target_a`) resets to `(0, 15)` — an initial 100-unit lead ahead of the craft — reads the same arrow input at `±7`, and on touching `frame_t` steps back and broadcasts `target_t`, which the craft receives and uses to back down: the four-message coupling (`target_t`/`target_b`/`target_l`/`target_r`) is what bounds the reticle and stops the craft at the top, in place of any factor keeping a rigid lead on screen. The arcade→stage mapping is an axis swap with a sign flip (arcade lateral → stage-X; arcade forward → stage-Y inverted); the orientation is the spec's strong inference, not a labeled fact.
- Scratch evidence: the `±7` movement steps, the `(0, -85)` spawn, and the `frame_b`/`frame_l`/`frame_r` touch-corrections with their `target_b`/`target_l`/`target_r` broadcasts (and the `target_t` receiver) in `solvalou_blocks`; the `DY 20` blaster clone travel in `blaster_blocks`; the input-driven crosshair, its `frame_t` clamp + `target_t` broadcast, and its `target_b`/`target_l`/`target_r` receivers in `target_blocks("target_a", 15)`; the recovery-build movement/crosshair contracts (W3, P1) and their negatives in `tests/test_scratch_project.py`; the port-conversion rules in the [core game systems](../spec/core-game-systems.md) Units section.
- Acceptance criteria: The craft moves at the recorded per-axis step, spawns at the fixed point, and is bounded by the frame touch with the crosshair-following broadcasts; the shot travels at the recorded speed; the crosshair reads input, leads the craft, and is clamped-and-coupled at the top border so it cannot leave the screen (the craft stopping with it); the recovery-build negatives prove each contract reddens when corrupted; two clean builds stay byte-identical and survive the build→import round-trip. The on-screen half — that the movement, bounds, shot speed, and crosshair coupling feel right — is the operator playtest.
- Fidelity status: **Applied (movement, shot flight, frame-touch bounds, crosshair coupling).** The recorded per-axis speed, spawn, frame bounds, shot speed, and the input-driven crosshair with its top-border coupling are the validated recovery-build behavior, unchanged. Deferred: the bomb-targets-crosshair coupling (the reticle's true relation to the bomb-drop point) and the crosshair's *lock-over-target* half to the ground/bomb slices; the Bacura bounce (WPN-01) and collision-triggered death (PLY-02) to their slices. Part of #15.
- License status: The pinned reference repository states no reusable license; no reference source text or media was copied — the movement rule and constants are instruction-derived and the stage values are a derived port conversion.
- Known deviations or uncertainty: (1) The arcade-to-screen **axis orientation is a strong inference** (from the 224 clamp literal), carried as such, not ratified as labeled fact. (2) A **single spatial factor was tried and rejected**: mapping the arcade playfield onto the 480×360 stage at one stage-units-per-pixel scale either shrank the play area or pushed the crosshair off the top of the screen (the reticle left the frame and the craft's movement became too slow to play). The port therefore tunes movement speed, shot speed, and the crosshair lead **independently**, and bounds the reticle with the craft↔crosshair coupling — the recorded model in the core-game-systems Units section. (3) The **visible playfield frames** (`frame_*` overlay costumes) are the bound itself (the craft touches them), so they line up with the play area by construction; whether the crosshair's coupled top-stop sits exactly where the painted top border is remains an **operator visual confirmation** (no repository check runs a Scratch VM).
