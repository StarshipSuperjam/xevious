# Score-adaptive AI level — pressure that tracks how well the player is doing

- Mechanic: DIF-02 (partial, foundation) — the schedule's `adjust_ai_level_from_score` records re-tune the [AI level](019-difficulty-ai-level.md) from performance: a player scoring heavily per craft spent meets sharper pressure, one who has lost many craft is spared. It reads the live score ([ECO-01](009-scoring-path.md)) and, since slice 21, the per-player ship number ([record 056](056-release-fidelity.md) item 4).
- Derived behavior: While an area scrolls, each adjust record (21 across the sixteen areas, zero to four per area) adds to the AI level the player's score in thousands divided by the ship number, floored and capped at 16. Since slice 21 this is the arcade's arithmetic exactly ([record 056](056-release-fidelity.md) item 4): the ship number is the craft in play counted from 1 (not the craft left), the thousands are the score's BCD digits read as binary, the cap is a signed compare, a ship number of 0 gives 0xFFFF, and the add wraps in the byte. Unlike the raise record, this add is **not** folded back. The result feeds the same AI level every later enemy behaviour scales its pressure by.
- Reference provenance: `jotd666/xevious@71473685a8c7856c8401c8519276cd97a38d4183`; `src/xevious_sub.68k`: `sub_2_fn_23__adjust_AI_level_based_on_score` (344–353) — the score-in-thousands input, the `avg_score_per_solvalou` divide (360–372, dividing by `solvalou_number`, the craft-in-play count), the `cmp #16` cap, and the add to `enemy_AI_level`. The behaviour is the settled [difficulty and formations](../spec/difficulty-and-formations.md) document.
- Transfer class: General behavior (instruction-derived rule; no source text or media copied).
- Scratch interpretation: The adjust branch of the schedule consume (`_consume_schedule`) computes the addend into the transient `ai adjust` working register. Until slice 21 it divided `floor(score / 1000)` by the craft left, guarded on `craft > 0`, on the mistaken reading that `solvalou_number` counts the craft remaining. Since slice 21 it builds the BCD dividend, divides by `ship number` (0xFFFF when that is 0), caps at 16 unless the quotient is 0x8000 or more, and adds within the byte ([record 056](056-release-fidelity.md) item 4).

What is deferred, per the spec's own acceptance table: the operator-visible *difference* ("playing better = harder waves") needs the enemy waves the AI level scales, which arrive with the enemy slices (8+). **Slice 10 update (leaf #60):** the `.play` half is delivered — the un-folded score adjust is proven to push the live AI level past the raise-only fold ceiling, and DIF-02 is now **built** ([record 032](032-live-pressure.md)).

- Known deviations or uncertainty: Until slice 21 the divisor was the live `craft` count, read as matching the reference's `solvalou_number`; that reading was wrong (`solvalou_number` is the craft in play, counted up from 1), and slice 21 corrects it ([record 056](056-release-fidelity.md) item 4). The adjust does **not** fold back (per the reference), so it can push the AI level above 0x80 transiently; because the raise's re-select uses the *post-fold* level and a set-formation uses its own offset, the formation index stays in the table's domain. This is not left to inspection: `test_formation_index_in_domain_over_committed_schedules` now walks the real schedule with the adjust forced to its worst-case +16 and proves every selection index stays in −32..127. The margin holds because the committed schedule interleaves few consecutive adjusts between raises, so the raise's single −0x40 fold always recovers a < 0x80 index; a future schedule or DIP change that broke that margin reddens that fixture (the two-sided formation guard in [FORM-01](020-normal-flying-formations.md) is the fail-safe backstop). In normal play the level stays well below the byte wrap because raises fold it; since slice 21 the raise and the re-tune both add within the byte anyway, as the arcade's `add.b` does. The generated adjust's live execution rests on the model rule plus the structural dispatch check and the operator playtest: the Python block interpreter executes only `set`/`if` (not the `repeat until` consume), and the scratch-vm harness observes the AI level but cannot isolate an adjust from the raises in the same run — matching the spec's deferral of DIF-02's in-game half.
