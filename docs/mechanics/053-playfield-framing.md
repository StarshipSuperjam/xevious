# Playfield framing — the borderless stage, positional craft limits, row-0 shot expiry, and the in-view gate

- Mechanic: PRES-01 (playfield framing) — the 480×360 landscape stage carries **no border**. The four baseline border sprites (`frame_t`, `frame_b`, `frame_l`, `frame_r`) are removed; the craft is bounded by fixed positional stop lines instead of touching them; a player shot is retired as it passes arcade row 0 instead of on touching the top band; world objects are drawn only while their row is on the field (rows 0–39); and the drawn layers run craft-and-weapons over the HUD over the world objects.
- Derived behavior: The baseline frame sprites were 15-unit opaque bands at the top and bottom of the stage and 1-unit strips at the sides, and they moved themselves to the front layer every frame — so the top band covered the HUD label row (stage y 172), which the operator flagged, and nothing about the arcade calls for a landscape border. With them gone: (1) **Craft.** The arcade craft clamps its own position on both axes every frame (to rows 18–38). The port keeps the stop lines the frame sprites used to produce — the craft costume's extents (up 19.5, down 18, left/right 18 stage units at size 150) against the frame bands' opaque pixels, walked on the craft's ±7-unit movement lattice from its (0, −85) spawn — so the reachable area is unchanged: x within ±217, y from −141 up to 139. Each axis is now an `if beyond the line, set to the line` clamp; on the ±7 lattice from the spawn these are exactly the positions the touch-correction settled on. (2) **Shot.** The arcade moves the shot up by its fixed step every frame and deletes it once its depth coordinate reads at or past row 40 unsigned — which, for an upward shot, happens the moment it wraps below row 0, before it is drawn there. The port's shot records the position it was drawn at into its slot (the hit position the air detector reads, one tick behind the drawing as before), then moves; a move that carries it above stage y 155 (`RENDER_ROW_TOP`, the port's row 0) hides it before it can be drawn there, and the shot retires after that tick, freeing its slot for the next shot. So a shot is never drawn past row 0, yet its topmost drawn position is still hit-tested — the hit reach runs to row 0, as the arcade's does — and a shot fired from the craft's top stop (row 2, where its first step already passes row 0) is hit-tested once at its spawn row, unseen, rather than retired untested. (3) **World objects.** The arcade culls an object once it reaches row 40 at the bottom or row −2 off the top (row −1 is still live) — the same routine also culls it past the side edge, which the port's object walks already do — and its visible screen shows rows 4–39. Every slot-driven world renderer — the ten flying families, the enemy bullets, the shared ground pool (including the Andor Genesis parts), and the Bonus Flag — shows its clone only while `0 <= slot x < 40 × 256` (rows 0–39) and hides it otherwise. The gate only decides visibility; it never writes a slot list, so game logic and hit resolution are unchanged. (4) **Layers.** The reference gives only the craft body's hardware sprites priority over the enemy objects and redraws its foreground text layer over them; the port draws, front to back, the craft and its weapons (the craft, crosshair, and bomb front every tick; a shot fronts at its creation and the explosion when it starts), then the HUD (its clones front once at creation), then the world objects, which no longer front themselves and instead sit at a fixed layer band: Bonus Flag 26 < ground pool 27 < Zakato 28, Giddo Spario 29, Brag Spario 30, Garu Zakato 31, Bacura 32, Sheonite 33, Toroid 34, Terrazi 35, Kapi 36, Torkan 37, Zoshi 38, Jara 39 < enemy bullets 40. The SEC-03 hidden-credit overlay keeps its own front (layer 42).

- Scratch interpretation: `expected_project` prunes the four `FRAME_TARGETS` (as the slice-13 ground merge pruned its legacy targets) and pins `layerOrder` for every target in `WORLD_RENDER_LAYER_ORDERS`. `solvalou_blocks` replaces the four `touching frame_*` corrections with four positional clamps against `CRAFT_Y_TOP` (139), `CRAFT_Y_BOTTOM` (−141) and `±CRAFT_X_LIMIT` (217), each an `if (position > / < limit) then set position to limit`. `blaster_blocks` orders the shot's travel-loop body as mirror-position, move, next-costume, then `if y position > RENDER_ROW_TOP then hide`, and ends the loop on `y position > RENDER_ROW_TOP` or the existing spent flag, in place of `touching frame_t` — so the clone is hidden before any draw above row 0, its last drawn position stays in the slot for the hit test on the hidden final tick, and it then frees its slot and deletes itself. A shared helper `_gate_in_view` wraps each world renderer's per-tick body in `if (not (slot x < 0)) and (slot x < RENDER_VIEW_ROWS × 256) then <render> else hide`; for the shared ground pool the per-tick `clear graphic effects` stays outside the gate. The per-tick `go to front layer` is removed from the eleven world renderers that had it. The two background terrain strips (`area_01a`, `area_01b`, 360 tall, each moving down 1 a tick from y 345 to Scratch's fence at y −345 and wrapping after 690 steps, so a strip's y is always 345 − its step) are re-seeded exactly half a cycle apart — `area_01a` at y −15 / step 360, `area_01b` at y 330 / step 15 — so the pair is always 345 apart and covers the whole stage on every tick. The baseline seeds (y −15 / step 355, y 344 / step 0) broke that law, so the spacing alternated between 336 and 355 and a black gap of up to 10 units opened at the bottom edge, then half a cycle later at the top edge, about every 11 seconds; the retired 15-unit frame bands had hidden it, and the operator saw it flash at playtest once they were gone. The opening screen is unchanged.

- Known deviations or uncertainty: (1) **Whole-sprite hiding (port necessity).** Scratch cannot clip a sprite at a screen edge, and with no border nothing masks an object above the field, so an object is hidden whole once its row leaves rows 0–39, rather than sliding partly off the screen edge. (2) **Row-0 cut line instead of row 4 (deviation).** The arcade shows rows 4–39; the port's render map puts row 4 at stage y 123, and the port already uses the strip above it (the HUD at y 128/155/172, and the craft's top stop near row 2), so a rows-4–39 cut would leave a band of live, invisible enemies the craft can fly into. The cut is at row 0 (y 155), operator-chosen. (3) **Stop-line values (retained deviation).** The arcade bounds the craft to rows 18–38; the port keeps the reach the baseline frame sprites gave it (about rows 2–37 in depth and the full width), as the locked units decision tunes spatial quantities independently. The values are the old frame stop lines, not derived from the arcade clamp. (4) **Crosshair at the top stop (pre-existing, unchanged).** With the craft at its top stop the crosshair's fixed lead points above the stage, so Scratch's own stage fence holds it against the top edge; the crosshair is still never clamped by the game. (5) **Layer band is a static order.** Scratch has no per-object draw priority, so the reference's first-pass ground / later-pass flyer order is encoded as one fixed layer per target; within one target, clones draw in creation order. (6) **Weapons over the HUD (owner choice).** In the reference only the craft body is a hardware sprite above everything; the crosshair, bomb, bomb target, and player shots are BOBs drawn in the object layer, under the redrawn text, and the enemy bullets (later object slots) draw after the player shots. The port fronts the player group above the HUD and the enemy bullets — the craft, crosshair, and bomb every tick, a shot at its creation, the explosion when it starts (so a HUD piece re-created mid-flight, such as a life icon, can land over an in-flight shot). The operator chose this order (the slice-20 framing decision: "the craft (and its shots, reticle, bomb) still never hidden") so the craft and its weapons are never hidden; the visible consequence is that with the craft at its top stop the crosshair covers the score text instead of sitting under it.
